We present Learning to Explain (LTX), a model-agnostic framework designed to provide post hoc explanations for vision models. LTX introduces an explainer model that produces explanation maps by identifying the most relevant regions of an input image that support the predictions made by a given model. The explainer is trained in two stages: an initial pretraining phase followed by per-instance fine-tuning.

In both training stages, we leverage a novel setup that compares the predictions of the target model on masked versus unmasked inputs. This enables a counterfactual training objective, where the explainer learns to anticipate the model’s behavior using perturbed (masked) versions of the input image. Crucially, LTX is architecture-agnostic and applicable to both Transformer-based and convolutional networks. Empirical results show that LTX achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple explainability benchmarks.

In this work, we extend the LTX framework by incorporating inpainting during the training phase as a replacement for traditional masking. Instead of simply masking out regions of the input, we inpaint them to generate more realistic counterfactuals. The explainer will now be trained using an inpainting-based perturbation metric.

To construct binary masks, we propose sampling from a Bernoulli distribution. A key challenge is to prevent the inpainting of irrelevant or uninformative pixels, which may dilute the effectiveness of the counterfactual supervision. We ask the model to reason about strategies that minimize inpainting of such non-relevant regions while maintaining training signal quality for the explainer.
